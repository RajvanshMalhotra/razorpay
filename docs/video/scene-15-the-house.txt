SCENE 15 - WHAT THE PROCESSOR SEES
One 15 second clip.

Two corrections from the log, both of which make the scene
stronger rather than weaker.


VISUAL

Part one, about 6 seconds.

Start on one stick figure alone behind its counter, exactly as
the film opened. The camera pulls back slowly and steadily.

More figures come into frame as it pulls back, each behind its
own counter. The pull-back ends on a field of thirty-two. Not
hundreds. Do not keep zooming out into a swarm.

Thin yellow lines are already drawn between them, connecting
pairs that have traded. The web is sparse and uneven - some
figures have four lines, some have one. It should look like a
real market, not a snowflake.

Part two, about 9 seconds.

Above the field, on a raised horizontal line, three figures walk
into frame one at a time and stand looking down over everything.
They are drawn slightly larger than the merchants.

  The first carries a folded chart.
  The second carries a ledger.
  The third stands with one arm held straight out - the same
  barrier pose from the gate scene.

The third figure is the one the audience has already seen. Hold
a beat longer on it.


ON SCREEN

  During the pull-back:
    32 businesses - 36 trading relationships

  As each of the three arrives, beside it:
    RESEARCH
    ACCOUNTANT
    THE GATE


VOICEOVER

  "Pull back far enough and a pattern appears that no single
  business on the floor can see."

  (as the three arrive)

  "So the processor runs three of its own. One reads the whole
  market. One keeps everybody honest against Razorpay. And one
  stands in front of every rupee before it moves."


FINAL FRAME

The thirty-two below, the three above, the yellow web between.

Large handwritten text:

  ONE FLOOR, ONE RECORD


ANIMATION DETAIL

The pull-back is one continuous move at a constant speed. It
stops. It does not accelerate into an infinite zoom, which is the
single most generated-looking move available and would also be a
lie about the size of the run.

Keep the merchant figures identical to the ones in Scene 1 and 2.
This is the same floor, seen from higher up.


WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY

The draft said "ten become hundreds". The run has thirty-two
merchants. In a film whose whole argument is that the numbers are
real, an establishing shot that inflates the population is the
cheapest possible thing to be caught on - and thirty-two is
plenty to look like a market.

The draft named the three as RESEARCH, ACCOUNTANT, AUCTIONEER.
The log has exactly three non-merchant actors and they are
'house', 'accountant' and 'gate'. Auctioning is one of the jobs
'house' does, alongside research and ranking - not a separate
agent. The gate is a separate actor, with 74 events of its own.

So the third figure should be the gate. That is the better scene
anyway: it puts the thing the track actually grades into the
establishing shot, and it pays off the barrier pose the audience
already learned two scenes earlier.

If you want to gesture at Razorpay's real scale, do it as its own
beat with its own honest label - never by quietly multiplying the
figures in this one.
