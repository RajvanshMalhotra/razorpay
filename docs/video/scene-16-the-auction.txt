SCENE 16 - TRADING MAKES INTELLIGENCE, INTELLIGENCE SELLS
One 15 second clip. Events 833 to 852.

Sealed envelopes, not raised hands. This is the whole point of
the scene and the reason the bidding is honest.


VISUAL

Part one, about 4 seconds.

The Research Agent stands at a table. Small yellow dots drift in
from the edges of the frame and gather in its hands. Count them
loosely, but keep it clearly a modest number - about sixteen, not
a swarm. The dots press together into a single card.

The card is blank. No text is generated.

Part two, about 7 seconds.

The Auctioneer sets the card down on the table.

Eight stick figures stand well apart from each other, each facing
front, each at its own small desk. None of them look at each
other. This separation must read clearly.

Each figure writes on a slip of paper, folds it once, and drops
it into a box at the centre. They do this at the same time, not
in turn.

The Auctioneer tips the box out. Eight folded slips spill across
the table at once.

Part three, about 4 seconds.

The Auctioneer slides the card to one figure. That figure slides
a small stack of yellow tokens back.

Then, without pause, the Auctioneer turns and pushes one single
token toward each of sixteen small figures standing at the back
of the frame, who had nothing to do with the bidding. They are
drawn faintly, as outlines only, with no faces at all.


ON SCREEN

  Over part one:
    833 - built from 16 businesses, none of them named

  As the slips spill out, all eight numbers appear at once,
  scattered, not in order:
    600   1500   200   300   500   1500   1200   1000

  Over part three:
    843 - sold for 1,500
    16 contributors paid, still unnamed


VOICEOVER

  "The trading itself produces something none of the traders can
  see. Sixteen businesses, and a pattern across all of them."

  (as the slips go into the box)

  "Eight agents bid for it, sealed. Nobody sees anybody else's
  number, so the only sensible strategy is an honest one."

  (as the tokens go back to the faint figures)

  "It sold for fifteen hundred. And the sixteen businesses whose
  trading produced it were each paid a share - without ever being
  named."


FINAL FRAME

The buyer holds the card. The sixteen faint outlines at the back
each hold one token.

Large handwritten text:

  YOUR TRADING IS THE PRODUCT

Beat. Then underneath, smaller:

  and you are paid for it


ANIMATION DETAIL

The eight bidders must never look at each other and must never
raise a hand. If they appear to react to one another, the scene
now says the opposite of what it should: an open auction rewards
gaming your rivals, a sealed one rewards saying what the thing is
actually worth to you.

All eight slips land at the same moment. Do not reveal them one
at a time and do not build to the highest.

The sixteen contributors are outlines with no faces on purpose.
They are paid without being identified, and that is visible in
the drawing rather than stated.

The lot is about micro-payment adoption spreading across a
widening set of merchants. It is NOT the electronics campaign -
that is a separate thing the research desk ranks on the internal
board, and it was never auctioned.

Two bids tied at 1500, so the second price equals the first here.
"The winner paid the runner-up's price" is true, but it is not
visible in this run. Do not build the scene around it.
