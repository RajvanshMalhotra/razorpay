AGENT EXCHANGE - VOICEOVER SCRIPT
Plain ASCII. Short lines. Nothing here needs assembling.
Every number is read from the run log and is correct as written.
==================================================================

VO 1  (over shots 1A and 1B)

Every business needs suppliers, and needs buyers. Finding them,
checking whether they are real, and arguing the price takes time a
small business does not have.

So most of them trade with whoever they already know, at whatever
price is listed.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 2  (over shot 2)

Razorpay already sits between all of them. It already knows who is
real and who pays.

So we built the Agent Exchange. Every merchant gets an agent. It
finds who to deal with on that network, argues the price down,
settles on real Razorpay payments, and writes the books.

And every rupee it moves is checked against a limit you set, ruled
on before it moves, and written to a log you can read back.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 2b  (over the first screen recording)

This is a real run. Thirty-two businesses. Nine hundred and
sixty-eight recorded events. Not one figure on screen was typed by
hand.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 3  (over shot 3)

This is what that looks like. Two agents, arguing about price, in
plain English.

VO 3b  (over the negotiation screen recording)

And these are their actual words, quoted from the log. Five rounds,
closing from two hundred rupees a unit to a hundred and eighty-five.

VO 3c  (over the network ring screen recording)

Thirty-two businesses. Thirty-six trading relationships. And nobody
made a single introduction.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 4  (shot 4 plays under the first line, then cut to screen)

Every step carries the number of the event it came from, so you can
look any of it up.

Event two hundred and five. The merchant wants a hundred and sixty
units of cold brew.

Event two hundred and seventeen. Its agent shortlisted three sellers
and argued for one.

Event two hundred and fifty-six. Agreed at a hundred and ninety-five
rupees a unit.

Event two hundred and fifty-eight. And here is the part that
matters. The gate refused it. A hundred and sixty units came to
thirty-one thousand two hundred rupees, over the twenty thousand
rupee cap on any single payment.

So the agent came back for twenty-five units at the same price. Not a
worse deal. A smaller first one, with a supplier it had never used.

The refusal is recorded. Not the outcome. The ruling, before any
money moved.

Event two hundred and sixty-one. Four thousand eight hundred and
seventy-five rupees, on a real Razorpay order.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 5  (shot 5, then the screen recording)

Then it broke.

Event seven hundred and sixty-three. Razorpay said that payment was
captured. Our books said pending. Nobody was watching. The
accountant found it on a routine sweep.

Event nine hundred and thirty-six. It froze that one merchant. Not
the market. One business's disagreement must not stop everybody
else.

Event nine hundred and thirty-seven. It repaired the settlement
using the payment id Razorpay gave back. Not one we invented. A
repair that invents an id is a machine for asserting payments that
never happened.

Event nine hundred and thirty-eight. And it let the merchant trade
again. A freeze that never lifts is a ban, not a hold.

Seventeen mismatches were caught and repaired this way. No human
touched any of them.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 6  (over shot 6)

You can only be in one meeting at a time. Your agent cannot.

VO 6b  (over the four agent cards and the brief control)

It is four parts with separate memories. Three act. The fourth only
watches, and remembers.

And you tell it how to behave, in plain words. That sets its
priorities. It can never set its permissions. No brief here can
raise a spending limit.

VO 6c  (over the Google Sheet)

The books keep themselves, into the merchant's own Google Sheet.

Eleven thousand five hundred and seventy spent. And the number only
the agent knows: a thousand and fifty saved, because the supplier
listed at two hundred and ten and the agent settled at a hundred and
ninety-five.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 7  (shot 7, then the desk screen recording)

The processor sees what no single merchant can. Which campaigns are
climbing across the whole client base, ranked from the log, with the
press that explains each one.

That intelligence sells by sealed auction. Eight agents bid. The
winner paid the runner-up's price. And the sixteen businesses whose
trading produced the insight were each paid a royalty, without ever
being named.

------------------------------------------------------------------
VO 8  (over shot 8)

Payments told you a transaction happened.

This tells you who should be doing business. And proves every rupee
of it.

CLOSING CARD  (black type on white, held three seconds)

968 events . 74 rulings recorded . 17 repairs . 0 humans
