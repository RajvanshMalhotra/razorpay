THE AGENT EXCHANGE - VIDEO SCRIPT

Style for every scene: 2D hand-drawn stick figure animation on a
plain white background. Black ink lines with uneven hand-drawn
weight. Simple round heads, dot eyes, no faces. Flat, no shading,
no gradients, no 3D. One accent colour only, a warm yellow.
Locked-off camera. Calm, plain, confident tone. No stock footage,
no corporate imagery, no people.


SCENE 1

VISUAL: One stick figure stands alone behind a small counter. It
looks left, then right. Nobody comes. It shrugs.

ON SCREEN: Every business needs suppliers. Finding them, checking
them, and arguing the price takes time nobody has.

NARRATION: Every business needs suppliers, and needs buyers.
Finding them, checking whether they are real, and arguing the
price takes time a small business does not have.


SCENE 2

VISUAL: Wide shot of forty tiny stick figures, each alone behind
its own small counter, evenly spaced. None of them face each
other. They are completely still.

ON SCREEN: So most trade with whoever they already know, at
whatever price is listed.

NARRATION: So most of them trade with whoever they already know,
at whatever price is listed.


SCENE 3

VISUAL: The same grid of forty figures. A single warm yellow line
draws itself from one figure to another. Then a second line, then
several more, until a sparse web connects most of them.

ON SCREEN: Razorpay already sits between all of them.

NARRATION: Razorpay already sits between all of them. It already
knows who is real and who pays.


SCENE 4

VISUAL: Full white frame. The words appear, and a warm yellow
rule draws itself underneath, left to right.

ON SCREEN: THE AGENT EXCHANGE

NARRATION: So we built the Agent Exchange. Every merchant gets an
agent. It finds who to deal with on that network, argues the
price down, settles on real Razorpay payments, and writes the
books. And every rupee it moves is checked against a limit you
set, ruled on before it moves, and written to a log you can read
back.


SCENE 5

VISUAL: SCREEN RECORDING. The exchange landing page loading, with
a yellow highlighter drawing across the headline.

ON SCREEN: read from the event log - nothing here was typed by hand

NARRATION: This is a real run. Thirty-two businesses. Nine
hundred and sixty-eight recorded events. Not one figure on screen
was typed by hand.


SCENE 6

VISUAL: SCREEN RECORDING. A ring of thirty-two merchant names
with lines drawn between the ones that traded. Two of them light
up in turn.

ON SCREEN: 1 - It finds who to deal with

NARRATION: Thirty-two businesses. Thirty-six trading
relationships. And nobody made a single introduction.


SCENE 7

VISUAL: Two stick figures face each other in profile. Empty
speech bubbles pop above them in turn, left then right, each one
slightly smaller than the last. Then cut to a SCREEN RECORDING of
the real quoted offers scrolling.

ON SCREEN: 2 - It argues the price
Then, in the speech bubbles as they pop: 200, 205, 190, 195, 180,
190, 170, 185

NARRATION: This is what that looks like. Two agents, arguing
about price, in plain English. And these are their actual words,
quoted from the log. Five rounds, closing from two hundred rupees
a unit to a hundred and eighty-five.


SCENE 8

VISUAL: A stick figure offers a small yellow parcel to another. A
third figure stands between them with one arm held straight out
like a barrier. The parcel stops at the arm and waits. The arm
lowers. The parcel passes. Then hold on a SCREEN RECORDING of the
audit trail, one station at a time.

ON SCREEN: 3 - Every rupee is ruled on first

NARRATION: Every step carries the number of the event it came
from, so you can look any of it up. Event two hundred and five,
the merchant wants a hundred and sixty units of cold brew. Event
two hundred and seventeen, its agent shortlisted three sellers
and argued for one. Event two hundred and fifty-six, agreed at a
hundred and ninety-five rupees a unit. Event two hundred and
fifty-eight, and here is the part that matters. The gate refused
it. The supplier was unknown and the amount was over the cap for
a first deal. So the agent came back smaller, and that was
allowed. The refusal is recorded. Not the outcome. The ruling,
before any money moved. Event two hundred and sixty-one, four
thousand eight hundred and seventy-five rupees, on a real
Razorpay order.


SCENE 9

VISUAL: A stick figure holds an open ledger. Beside it a second
figure holds an identical ledger with different scribbles. The
first notices and freezes mid-step with one foot raised. A third
figure walks in, takes both ledgers, makes a single mark, and
hands them back matching. The frozen figure puts its foot down
and walks on. Then a SCREEN RECORDING of the same audit trail
continuing.

ON SCREEN: 4 - It catches its own mistakes

NARRATION: Then it broke. Event seven hundred and sixty-three,
Razorpay said that payment was captured, our books said pending.
Nobody was watching. The accountant found it on a routine sweep.
Event nine hundred and thirty-six, it froze that one merchant.
Not the market. One business's disagreement must not stop
everybody else. Event nine hundred and thirty-seven, it repaired
the settlement using the payment id Razorpay gave back. Not one
we invented. Event nine hundred and thirty-eight, and it let the
merchant trade again. A freeze that never lifts is a ban, not a
hold. Seventeen mismatches were caught and repaired this way. No
human touched any of them.


SCENE 10

VISUAL: The frame is split by a thin vertical line. On the left a
stick figure lies asleep and completely still. On the right the
same figure, drawn in warm yellow, walks, hands over a parcel,
and shakes hands with another figure. Then a SCREEN RECORDING of
a spreadsheet filling itself in.

ON SCREEN: 5 - The books keep themselves

NARRATION: You can only be in one meeting at a time. Your agent
cannot. You tell it how to behave in plain words. That sets its
priorities. It can never set its permissions. And the books keep
themselves, into the merchant's own Google Sheet. Eleven thousand
five hundred and seventy spent, and a thousand and fifty saved,
because the supplier listed at two hundred and ten and the agent
settled at a hundred and ninety-five.


SCENE 11

VISUAL: Many small stick figures along the bottom of the frame,
each looking down at its own tiny counter. High above them one
figure stands on a raised horizontal line and looks out across
all of them. Warm yellow lines connect the figures below into a
shape only the raised figure can see.

ON SCREEN: 6 - What only Razorpay can see

NARRATION: The processor sees what no single merchant can. Which
campaigns are climbing across the whole client base, ranked from
the log. That intelligence sells by sealed auction. Eight agents
bid, and the winner paid the runner-up's price. The sixteen
businesses whose trading produced the insight were each paid a
royalty, without ever being named.


SCENE 12

VISUAL: The full grid of forty stick figures, now densely
connected by steady warm yellow lines. Every figure turns to face
the centre together in one motion. Hold, then fade to white.

ON SCREEN: 968 events - 74 rulings recorded - 17 repairs - 0 humans

NARRATION: Payments told you a transaction happened. This tells
you who should be doing business. And proves every rupee of it.
