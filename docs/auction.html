One insight, sold once.

Razorpay mints a lot from what it can see across every client, sells it by sealed bid, and pays a royalty to the merchants whose trading produced it — without naming any of them.

What was sold

“Micro-payment adoption is expanding, driven by a fragmentation of one-off, low-value settlements across a broadening merchant base.”

The headline above is free and published to everyone. What the winner actually bought is the detail underneath it — the ranked campaigns, how far each has spread, and the threads each was counted from.

Why the winner paid less than it bid

The top bid wins and pays the second price. Bidding your true value can then never cost you more than the thing is worth to you, so nobody has to guess at rivals — and the reasoning in that table stays legible instead of becoming a bluff.

Who got paid

16 merchants each earned 28 points from this sale. Their trading produced the insight; none of them was named in it, and none knew it was being sold. That is the whole shape of the product: the platform sees what no single client can, sells it once, and pays the clients whose activity made it.
